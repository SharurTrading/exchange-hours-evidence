11 October 2016
Singapore’s Semiconductor Stocks in Focus as Industry Rebounds
  • Global semiconductor sales are showing signs of rebound, with the manufacturing output of Singapore’s semiconductors segment continuing its YoY growth in August. The segment’s YoY output growth in the year-to-date – at 19.7% – is highest amongst all the sectors in Singapore’s manufacturing industry.
  • Singapore-listed semiconductor stocks averaged a total return of 12.4% in the YTD, outperforming the FTSE ST All Share Index’s total return of 5.4% in the same period. The three best-performing semiconductor stocks over the period were AEM Holdings (+71.7%), Sunright (+56.1%) and Avi-Tech Electronics (+53.0%).
  • The five largest semiconductor stocks in Singapore by market capitalisation are UMS Holdings, Micro-Mechanics, Ellipsiz, CDW Holding, and Avi-Tech Electronics. These five stocks averaged a 18.0% total return in the year thus far and maintain an average dividend yield of 6.8%.

Based on data provided by The Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA), semiconductor sales globally reached US$28.0 billion (+3.5% month-on-month and +0.5% year-on-year) in August 2016. Notably, the MoM growth in August was the market’s largest since May 2013 and its first YoY growth since June 2015, according to the SIA. SIA’s President & CEO noted that the global semiconductor market has recently 
shown signs of rebound – as evidenced by the solid growth in August – following months of sluggish sales.

 

While global sales are still behind last year’s, the semiconductor market appears to be on the right track as 2017 approaches. SIA expects the global semiconductor market in 2016 to decline 2.4% to US$327.2 billion from previous year’s levels. The strength in semiconductors is also in line with the North American SEMI book-to-bill ratio*, which has remained above 1.0 since December 2015, for nine consecutive months. Beyond the year, the SIA expects the market to grow modestly at 2.0% YoY in 2017.

 

The strength seen in the global semiconductor market was also reflected in Singapore’s manufacturing output data. According to data published by the Economic Development Board (EDB), Singapore’s total manufacturing output increased 0.1% YoY (or +2.3% YoY excluding biomedical manufacturing) in August 2016. The output of Singapore’s Electronics cluster, which accounts for 27.4% of total manufacturing activity, rose 10.8% YoY in August, following 16.5% YoY growth in the previous month. The semiconductors segment was the key contributor, with output rising 19.7% YoY in August, following a 34.4% increase in July. The Machinery & Systems segment (under the Precision Engineering cluster) also benefited from higher output in semiconductor-related equipment, growing 17.1% YoY in August after a 1.2% YoY decline the previous month. 

                                        

There are 12 Semiconductor and Semiconductor Equipment stocks as categorised by the Global Industry Classification System (GICS®) listed on the Singapore Exchange (SGX). These 12 companies have a combined market capitalisation of S$731 million. The five largest capitalised stocks among the 12 are: UMS Holdings, Micro-Mechanics, Ellipsiz, CDW Holding, and Avi-Tech Electronics. These five stocks averaged a 18.0% total return in the year-to-date. The five stocks also maintain an average dividend yield of 6.8%, above the FTSE ST All Share Index’s yield of 3.9%.

 

In the 2016 year-to-date, these 12 semiconductor stocks averaged a total return of 12.4%, outperforming the FTSE ST All Share Index’s total return of 5.4% over the same period.

*The North American SEMI book-to-bill ratio is part of a monthly report published by SEMI®, tracking worldwide billings and bookings of North American-headquartered manufacturers of equipment used to make semiconductor devices. The book-to-bill ratio is the ratio of bookings (i.e. orders) received and billed for a specific period, and is derived by dividing total bookings by total billings. A ratio above 1 implies more orders are received than billed, suggesting stronger demand, and vice versa when the ratio falls below 1.
