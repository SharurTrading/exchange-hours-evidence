Nasdaq raises red flag on 3 overnight risks ahead of Dec. 6 — and they pose an extra-big threat to retirement nest eggs

Christy Bieber
October 24, 2026 7 min read
  • ^IXIC

Moneywise and Yahoo Finance LLC may earn commission or revenue through links in the content below.

Estimates suggest there are around 450,000 day traders (1) in the United States. Foreign investors also held more than $3.2 trillion in U.S. equities (2) in recent years.

As day trading, active stock trading, and foreign interest in U.S. markets have grown, demand for more trading hours has increased, and exchanges have delivered.

The Nasdaq (3) recently announced it will introduce new trading hours beginning December 6, 2026. Those hours will allow continuous trading 23 hours a day, five days a week. The NYSE is also following suit, announcing pre- and post-market trading sessions (4) for select investments.

More trading opportunities may feel like a boon to investors, but they're actually a huge threat to those nearing or in retirement. There are three primary reasons why that's the case.

1. Active trading is riskier

The Securities and Exchange Commission (5) indicates day trading can be "highly risky," and that traders must "be prepared to suffer severe financial loss." And it's not just day trading either. Any type of short-term investing presents inherent dangers compared with long-term investing.

Active trading is riskier because short-term price movements can be unpredictable, and transaction costs can be high. You need to earn more to cover commissions, fees, and potential capital gains taxes you may pay regularly, and it's harder to do that because timing the market is challenging.

Extended hours provide more opportunities to trade on vibes or breaking news, the impact of which isn't yet fully known. As a result, these extra trading hours can lead to questionable choices that result in outsized losses.

2. Overnight trading increases volatility

Overnight trading likely adds risks beyond simply prompting you to buy stock on a whim at 4 a.m.. There are likely to be fewer active buyers participating in trades during non-business hours.

With lower participation and thinner order books, liquidity is reduced. Even a small buy or sell order could cause a wide spread between the bid (the price the buyer wants to pay) and the ask (the price the seller wants to collect).

Erratic price swings and substantial losses could result.

3. Retirees have less time to recover from mistakes

These risks may affect all traders who want to take advantage of new extended trading hours to trade more actively. But retirees could be hit especially hard because they don't have time to recover from mistakes or bad luck, as they'll be relying on their nest egg to cover costs.

Losses during early retirement can have an outsized impact on whether your money lasts, as you may be forced to sell to support yourself before your investments have had enough time to benefit from compound growth. In simple terms, if you lose money today, you'll be much worse off than if that money earned you returns for 15 years before you lose it.

The risk of early losses is called sequence of returns risk, and it can devastate your portfolio if you have a string of bad trades shortly after retiring. When you're forced to make withdrawals to cover the bills, you'll lock in losses, and the money that should have been working for you will be forever gone — leaving you with a smaller balance to continue growing and supporting you.

Of course, you don't have to take these risks. Just because there will soon be more opportunities to invest in equities overnight or outside business hours doesn't mean you have to seize them.

Instead, follow tried-and-true investing best practices and make sound investments that can help you maximize your chances of achieving the retirement security you deserve. Here's what you can do.

Invest automatically in ETFs

ETF investing is a lower-risk approach that lets you invest without much advanced knowledge. And the beauty of ETF investing is its accessibility — anyone, regardless of wealth, can take advantage of it. Even small amounts can grow over time

Acorns makes this process automatic. How it works is simple: Just sign up and link your debit or credit cards. Then, every time you buy something, Acorns rounds up the total to the nearest dollar. This turns a morning coffee for $3.25 into a 75-cent investment in your future.

From here, that money goes into a diversified portfolio of ETFs that you can tailor to your risk tolerance. Along with big names like the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF, Acorns also sets you up to invest in specific sectors of the economy through custom portfolios. This includes areas like heavy industry, energy, consumer staples and utilities.

Then, once you're comfortable with your round-ups, you can supercharge your savings with recurring monthly deposits into your favorite funds. And if you sign up today with a $5 recurring deposit, Acorns will add a $20 bonus to help you begin your investment journey.

Build a diversified portfolio that includes inflation hedges

While investing responsibly in equities can help you build wealth, you may also want to add some other assets to your portfolio for additional diversification. Traditionally, commercial real estate has served as a solid hedge against inflation and economic uncertainty.

Owning multi-family real estate could help reduce sequence of return risk by providing an opportunity to own assets that can provide a steady income stream and potentially perform well even during turbulent times.

Accredited investors can now tap into this opportunity through platforms such as Lightstone DIRECT, which gives accredited investors access to single-asset multifamily and industrial deals.

Lightstone DIRECT's direct-to-investor model ensures a high degree of alignment between individual investors and a vertically-integrated, institutional owner-operator — a sophisticated and streamlined option for individual investors looking to diversify into private-market real estate.

With Lightstone DIRECT, accredited individuals can access the same multifamily and industrial assets Lightstone pursues with its own capital, with minimum investments starting at $100,000.

Getting advice from a financial professional can also help you build a solid investment strategy that doesn't rely on speculative trading.

The platform also offers guidance on saving for retirement and lets you set additional goals as your life evolves.
