NYSE Arca and Nasdaq to Launch Extended Trading: What Companies Should Consider

Posted by Marc S. Gerber and Caroline S. Kim, Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP, on
Wednesday, October 21, 2026
Comment Print E-Mail Tweet
ET, NYSE, proposals, SEC
More from: Caroline Kim, Marc Gerber, Skadden

Marc S. Gerber is a Partner and Caroline S. Kim is a Counsel at Skadden, Arps, Slate, Meagher & Flom LLP.  This post is based on a Skadden memorandum by Mr. Gerber, Ms. Kim, Brian V. Breheny, Raquel Fox, Andrew J. Brady and Nicholas D. Lamparski, all at Skadden.

Executive Summary

  • What’s new: Beginning December 6, 2026, both the Nasdaq Stock Market and NYSE Arca are expected to extend trading hours to 23 hours per day, five days per week (23×5 trading).
  • Why it matters: The 23×5 trading expansion would add an overnight session from Sunday through Thursday from 9 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET, with a mandatory one-hour trading pause Monday through Thursday evening between 8 p.m. and 9 p.m. ET.
  • What to do next: Companies should consider the potential impacts on corporate policies and transaction documents that reference exchange-defined trading sessions and monitor disclosure practices in light of 23×5 trading.

__________

On April 10, 2026, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) approved a proposal by the Nasdaq Stock Market (Nasdaq) to extend trading hours to 23 hours per day, five days per week (23×5 trading). The SEC’s approval of Nasdaq’s proposal was followed by NYSE Arca’s amended rule filing submitted on May 12, 2026, describing the exchange’s operational framework for 23×5 trading. Both Nasdaq and NYSE Arca are targeting a December 6, 2026, launch of their extended 23×5 trading hours.

The exchanges currently support three trading sessions from Monday through Friday: 4 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. ET, 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET, and 4 p.m. to 8 p.m. ET. With the 23×5 trading expansion, Nasdaq and NYSE Arca would add an overnight session from Sunday through Thursday from 9 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET, with a mandatory one-hour trading pause Monday through Thursday evening between 8 p.m. and 9 p.m. ET. This mandatory trading pause enables the exchanges to conduct maintenance and testing and to process dividends, stock splits and other corporate actions pending for the next trading day. The trading week would begin with the night session on Sunday at 9 p.m. ET.

Only U.S. broker-dealers that are members of Nasdaq and NYSE Arca may trade during the overnight session. Retail investors or other firms may only trade on the overnight session through a member of each respective exchange. Trades executed between 9 p.m. and midnight ET will carry the next calendar day’s trade date, and settle one day later. Trades between midnight and 4 a.m. ET will carry that day’s trade date.

Trading Sessions Under Nasdaq and NYSE Arca

While Nasdaq’s and NYSE Arca’s changes result in 23×5 trading from 9 p.m. ET Sunday through 8 p.m. ET Friday, the two exchanges have structured their sessions differently. NYSE Arca will operate four distinct sessions, with an overnight session from 9 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET. NYSE Arca’s current timing of the Early, Core and Late Sessions will not change. By contrast, Nasdaq will operate two sessions only: a Night Session from 9 p.m. to 4 a.m. ET and a Day Session from 4 a.m. to 8 p.m. ET that consolidates what are now the Pre-Market Hours, Regular Market Hours and Post-Market Hours into a single session.

Certain core market conventions nevertheless will remain unchanged. For Nasdaq-listed companies, the traditional U.S. trading hours, from 9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET and marked by the Opening and Closing Crosses, will continue to set the prices to drive investor transparency across all hours of market operation. In addition, the regular market on NYSE Arca’s Core Session will continue to open at 9:30 a.m. ET, and the exchange will still publish its Official Closing Price following the Core Closing Auction at 4 p.m. ET.

Corporate Action Information

With the introduction of 23×5 exchange trading, there will only be a one-hour trading pause between 8 p.m. and 9 p.m. ET, which may not be sufficient for the processing of complex corporate actions across exchange and market participant systems. To address this, the primary listing markets will impose mandatory halts on the affected security for the duration of the overnight session until 8 a.m. ET on the effective date of the corporate action, effectively preserving the pre-23×5 status quo for these events.[1]

Practical Considerations for Companies

The move to extended trading raises several practical considerations for companies, including:

  • Insider trading and other corporate policies: Consider whether existing insider trading policies and trading window procedures require amendment in light of the new overnight session. For companies listed on the New York Stock Exchange, whose policies are tied to the preserved Core Session (9:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. ET), the overnight session may require little or no policy change. For Nasdaq-listed companies, the consolidation of pre-market, regular and post-market hours into a single Day Session (4 a.m. to 8 p.m. ET) may shift the meaning of policy terms like “market hours,” “market close” and “trading day.” Those companies should consider whether their insider trading policies and trading windows should reference specific hours rather than exchange-defined sessions.Nasdaq-listed companies also should consider reviewing other corporate policies and equity plans that reference exchange-defined sessions, which may have shifted under Nasdaq’s consolidated two-session structure. For instance, it may be advisable for companies to clarify how certain aspects of their equity plans or policies will be interpreted in light of 23×5 trading, particularly for Nasdaq-listed companies’ equity plans that reference exchange-defined sessions (e.g., using “closing sales price” in the Fair Market Value definition).
  • Disclosure practices: Longer trading days may have implications for how and when companies communicate material information to the market, including the timing of SEC report filings and press releases. For example, in his introductory remarks at the SEC’s “Roundtable on Preparations for 24-Hour Trading,” Chairman Paul Atkins encouraged issuers to provide feedback on how expanded trading hours may affect their obligations to disseminate material information to the market and the obligation to make SEC filings during EDGAR filing hours. Commissioner Hester Peirce echoed these comments in her opening remarks, noting that issuers currently make filings and publicize material information prior to or after core trading hours. She questioned whether companies may need to reconsider long-standing practices for timing material announcements as trading expands beyond core hours and whether the SEC may need to modify EDGAR to ensure that material information can be disseminated in a timely manner during the overnight session.
  • Transaction documents: Many agreements and offering documents contain terms that were drafted with traditional trading hours in mind. Companies should consider whether definitions, conditions and timing provisions would need to be modified for this new 23×5 trading environment. For instance, definitions of “trading day,” “market close” and “Volume-Weighted Average Price” may no longer work as intended under the 23×5 trading framework.
  • Market dynamics: Extended trading could change the landscape in which capital markets transactions are structured. Shifts in trading patterns and market behavior during nontraditional hours may affect transaction strategy and risk assessment.

What Happens Next

Several rules necessary to implement the 23×5 trading framework remain subject to SEC approval. Companies should expect further technical and fee-related filings as the December launch date approaches. Companies cannot opt out of their stock trading during the new 23×5 hours starting December 6, 2026, and should monitor developments related to 23×5 trading and prepare for their potential impact on corporate policies, documents and disclosure practices.

For more information, see Nasdaq’s Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) on Global Trading Hours and Corporate Action Mandatory Regulatory Halt Rules and NYSE Arca’s FAQ on Extended Hours Trading.
