#### Q: What is changing at Nasdaq?

* * *

Nasdaq is preparing to extend its U.S. equities market to operate 23 hours a day, five days a week, subject to DTCC and SIP readiness. This evolution is designed to meet growing global demand for access to U.S. markets while preserving the reliability, transparency, and investor protections expected from the world’s leading listing venue.

In preparation for this market evolution, Nasdaq is expanding market center coverage within its flagship market data solutions by expanding Nasdaq TotalView and Nasdaq Basic to include data from Nasdaq PSX and Nasdaq Texas. This expanded product set enables the flow of additional data, expands market coverage, and provides scalable market data infrastructure in anticipation of extended trading hours.

#### Q: What products are included in Nasdaq Plus Suite?

* * *

**Nasdaq Last Sale Plus**

Nasdaq Last Sale Plus has delivered consolidated trade reporting since 2010. It provides continuous last sale reporting across Nasdaq sessions and markets, supporting analytics, surveillance, and downstream systems that require uninterrupted trade data throughout the coming 23 hour trading day.

**Nasdaq Basic (BBO) Plus**

Nasdaq Basic Plus delivers best bid and offer visibility across extended trading hours, allowing clients to maintain a consistent BBO view as markets operate nearly around the clock. Clients can access both a consolidated view across Nasdaq’s exchanges and deeper order level insights when needed.

**Nasdaq TotalView® Plus**

Nasdaq TotalView Plus delivers depth of book data across the 23-hour trading day by integrating:

*   Nasdaq US and Global Session Depth

*   Nasdaq PSX depth

*   Nasdaq Texas depth

All order book data is delivered through a single, consolidated connection, simplifying consumption for trading and display applications.

This date is anticipated to align with the first day the Securities Information Processor (SIP) accepts and disseminates orders and trades over a 23-hour period, with Nasdaq launching its 23-hour trading day in parallel.

Nasdaq’s Plus market data products are launching ahead of this date to provide extended adoption opportunities in advance of the market hours expansion.

#### Q: How are timestamps represented when trading activity continues past midnight as part of the Global Trading Hours session? Do timestamps reset to zero at 12:00 AM?

* * *

No. Timestamps do **not** reset at calendar midnight during an active trading session.The timestamp field continues to represent the number of nanoseconds elapsed since the session's reference midnight and remains an unsigned integer. As a result, timestamp values will exceed 24 hours during the overnight portion of the trading session.

For example:

Event Time (ET)Timestamp Representation
Sunday 11:59:59.999999999 PM 23:59:59.999999999
Monday 12:00:00.000000000 AM 24:00:00.000000000
Monday 12:00:01.000000000 AM 24:00:01.000000000
Monday 1:00:01.000000000 AM 25:00:01.000000000

In other words, timestamps continue to increase sequentially throughout the trading session and are **not reset at midnight**. There are no negative timestamp values, and the timestamp field format and data type remain unchanged as part of the Global Trading Hours implementation.

#### Q: How are timestamps represented on the day that Daylight Saving Time begins or ends? Will timestamps be shifted by the DST clock change?

* * *

**A:** No. DST transitions do not cause timestamp values to gain or lose an hour within the Global Trading Hours session. Nasdaq timestamps continue to represent the elapsed nanoseconds since the session's reference midnight and remain sequential throughout the trading session. The timestamp calculation is based on the elapsed time from the reference midnight and is not adjusted when clocks move forward or backward for DST.

For example, on the spring-forward weekend:

Event Time (ET)Timestamp Representation
Sunday 8:00:00 PM 20:00:00.000000000
Monday 12:00:00 AM 24:00:00.000000000
Monday 4:00:00 AM 28:00:00.000000000

Although the spring-forward day contains only 23 civil-clock hours, the timestamps remain unchanged from what would be observed on a normal trading day. Similarly, on the fall-back weekend, timestamp values do not gain an extra hour.In other words, market participants should expect timestamp values to continue increasing monotonically throughout the session, regardless of DST transitions. This ensures consistent timestamp handling across midnight, overnight trading, and daylight saving time changes.

#### Q: Will a new Stock Directory (R) message be sent at approximately 4:00 AM ET when the market transitions from the Global Session to the U.S. Session?

* * *

A: Yes. For **Nasdaq TotalView Plus**, the Nasdaq market operates with two separate trading sessions:

*   **Session 1 (Global Session):**

9:00 PM ET to 4:00 AM ET

*   **Session 2 (U.S Session):**

4:00 AM ET to 8:00 PM ET

Clients should expect session-specific reference data, including Stock Directory messages, to identify the applicable Market/Session. These messages allow firms to correctly interpret market data associated with each Nasdaq session.

> **Note:** This question is specific to Nasdaq TotalView Plus, which maintains separate session-based order books. Nasdaq Last Sale Plus and Nasdaq Basic Plus (BBO Plus) operate as a continuous 23-hour trading day and do not have separate Session 1 and Session 2 order books.

#### Q: Will Stock Locate Codes remain the same between Regular Trading Hours and Global Trading Hours?

* * *

A: In most cases, clients should expect a security's Stock Locate Code to remain unchanged throughout the trading day. However, while it is expected to be rare, Nasdaq reserves the ability to assign a different Stock Locate Code when transitioning between Session 1 and Session 2 if extraordinary circumstances require it, such as a security being added during the trading day.

As a result, firms should not assume that a Stock Locate Code will remain constant across both sessions and should be prepared to process an updated Stock Directory message if a new locate assignment occurs.

#### Q: Are Stock Locate Codes unique, and can they overlap across Nasdaq, Nasdaq Texas (NTX), and PSX?

* * *

A: Stock Locate Codes are assigned independently by each market center. Therefore:

*   A Stock Locate Code is unique within a specific market and session.

*   The same numerical Stock Locate Code may be used by another market (Nasdaq, NTX, or PSX).

*   Clients should always use the combination of **Market/Session Indicator**and **Stock Locate Code**when identifying securities, rather than relying on the Stock Locate Code alone.

#### Q: Will there be any overlap between overnight and regular trading session orders?

* * *

**No.** The overnight trading session and the regular daytime trading session are separate markets. Before the daytime session begins, the overnight market closes and all remaining overnight orders are canceled. This ensures there is no overlap between overnight and daytime orders and helps prevent locked or crossed market conditions during the session transition.

#### Q: Will Nasdaq's expanded trading hours increase market data bandwidth requirements?

* * *

**No significant increase in bandwidth requirements is expected as a result of expanded trading hours.** The overnight and daytime sessions operate independently, with all overnight orders canceled before the regular trading session begins. As a result, Nasdaq does not anticipate additional throughput demands during the transition between sessions.
