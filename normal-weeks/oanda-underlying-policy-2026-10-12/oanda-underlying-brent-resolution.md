# Brent underlying-calendar resolution

Reviewed 2026-10-12 UTC. Read-only research: no runtime row, consumer file or issue was changed.

All 17 dates can be assigned a concrete **user-selected** policy: 13 retain OANDA ordinary sessions; 4 take ICE’s ordinary post-holiday restart. This closes the selected-policy decision, not the missing direct UK broker evidence. Direct OANDA statements always take precedence.

The donor is ICE Futures Europe Brent Crude Futures (product 219, B/BRN), not London cash equities. The retained OANDA static product guide, page 3 Commodity CFDs, expressly places Brent Crude Oil under “Underlying Futures Exchange: ICE EUROPE”. It is an undated earlier grid, used only to identify the donor; it does not date or overwrite current broker hours. [Operator guide](https://www.oanda.com/assets/documents/252/Hours_of_Operation.pdf).

The current [ICE Brent specification](https://www.ice.com/products/219/Brent-) gives ordinary London 01:00–23:00, Sunday 23:00 open, and New York 20:00–18:00. Retain OANDA’s London Sunday 23:01 open, Friday 21:59 close, weekday 21:59–22:05 break and 22:59–01:01 break. Settlement periods and marker calculation windows do not supply trading closes.

The JSON records all 17 proposed London and UTC executable blocks. Summer dates use BST/UTC+01:00 and EDT/UTC−04:00. Carter and Thanksgiving eve use GMT/UTC+00:00 and EST/UTC−05:00. These dates lie outside the US/UK DST mismatch periods:20:00 ET on the previous civil date is 01:00 London on the next date, or 00:00 UTC in summer. Ordinary broker 01:01 London is 00:01 UTC; its 21:59 close is 20:59 UTC. The weekday later block remains 22:05–22:59 London/21:05–21:59 UTC. Monday’s Sunday 23:01 London opening is 22:01 UTC on the previous date. Friday has no later block. The JSON handles Carter winter conversion explicitly.

The 13 normal-day proposals have positive annual-calendar context: UK Easter/May/Summer holidays are marked TD with an exhaustive named list of other contracts closed; the key defines TD as regular trading hours. July 3 and Thanksgiving eve are ordinary dates preceding the explicitly special dates. The 2026 schedule places the Singapore Labour Day marker adjustment on April 30, not a Brent closure on May 1. Carter is ordinary in the February 2025 annual calendar; [Circular 25/001](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/circulars/25001.pdf) changes only named SOFR/Swapnote products. This is strong underlying evidence, while inheriting it into a broker book remains a selected policy.

For the 4 reopens, the January 2024 calendar marks the previous US holiday C or D and states Brent trading ceases 18:30 London/13:30 ET. The next date is ordinary. Choosing 01:00 London is corroborated by OANDA’s explicitly named 2025 Memorial/Juneteenth/Labor Day 20:00 ET reopens and 2024 Thanksgiving 20:00 ET reopen, already captured in the shipped broker evidence. It still is not a direct statement of those four missing 2024 broker reopens. The recommendation uses a specific selected restart, not a blanket import of ICE’s normal phase grid.

Counterexamples prevent blind copying: ICE normally continues to 23:00 London while OANDA stops its later block 22:59 and its Friday session 21:59. The January 2024 ICE edition initially gave Thanksgiving-Friday key B a 19:00 London close, but the preserved November 2024 edition corrects B to 20:00 London/15:00 ET, agreeing with the explicit OANDA row. Use the later edition; the preliminary 19:00 cell is not a final broker/exchange disagreement. OANDA’s 2026 US-holiday Brent 13:29 ET closes are one minute earlier than the ICE C/D 13:30 ET closes. All directly stated broker minutes prevail. ICE’s listed UK bank holidays do not close Brent, so LSE holiday inheritance would incorrectly close these dates. OANDA’s omission language does not itself affirm normal trading.

The [October 2026 temporary DST notice](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/Trading_Schedule_Temporary_Trading_Hours_for_DST.pdf), page 2, explicitly prints Brent 00:00–22:00 GMT alongside 20:00–18:00 EDT for October 25–30. Thus 23:00 London is the ordinary printed close, not a universal year-round constant. None of these 17 gap dates is in a US/UK DST mismatch span. [ICE Circular 10/070](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/circulars/10070.pdf), page 1, independently witnesses the same 20:00–18:00 ET Brent grid in the 2010 temporary-DST period; it is context, not a newly invented historical cutover.
